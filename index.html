DSC 209R Final Project

Abortion Access and Travel Across U.S. States

Heta Joshi and Jade Romero

In 2020, abortion was legal in every U.S. state. But in Missouri, 99% of residents who obtained an abortion had to leave the state to get one.

Research question: How did access to abortion care differ across U.S. states before the Dobbs decision, and do high abortion counts tell the same story as access measures such as clinic availability and out-of-state travel?

Hover over a state on the map or in the ranking for its values. Click a state to keep it highlighted in both views, and click it again to clear.

Takeaway: legal did not mean accessible

Before the Dobbs decision, abortion was legal in every state, and states that provided the most abortions, like California and New York, did so largely because of their population. But access was very uneven: in states like Wyoming and Missouri, more than 9 in 10 women lived in a county with no clinic. Therefore, many residents of those states crossed state lines for care, while neighboring states like Illinois took in thousands of patients.

Raw counts hide this pattern. Linking the map to the ranking and the scatter plots shows that the states with the fewest nearby clinics are the same states where residents most often traveled out of state and where abortion rates among residents were lowest. These are associations across 51 states, not proof of cause, and the data does not track where individual patients went.
