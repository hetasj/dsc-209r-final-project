DSC 209R Final Project · Initial Prototype

Abortion Access and Travel Across U.S. States

Heta Joshi and Jade Romero

Research question: How did access to abortion care differ across U.S. states before the Dobbs decision, and do high abortion counts tell the same story as access measures such as clinic availability and out-of-state travel?

Hover over a state on the map or in the ranking for its values. Click a state to keep it highlighted in both views, and click it again to clear.

What we have done so far

We chose the Guttmacher Institute state-level abortion dataset and narrowed our focus to access: where abortions happen, where residents live, and how far people have to go for care. We cleaned the original workbook into a single CSV with one row per state and short column names, and we added a calculated measure, abortions provided minus residents' abortions, to show which states take in patients from other states and which send patients out. For our proposal, we made six static charts that compared abortion counts, residence-based rates, out-of-state travel, clinic deserts, and clinic changes from 2017 to 2020. This prototype turns those charts into one interactive view: a U.S. map colored by the selected measure, linked to a ranked bar chart of all 50 states and D.C. The measure menu, tooltips, and click-to-highlight already work, so a reader can switch between measures and follow one state across both views.

What will be the most challenging

The hardest design problem is that the dataset mixes counts, percentages, and rates from different years (2010 to 2020), so the measures cannot all be compared directly. We need to label every measure with its year and unit, and choose color scales that fit each one, such as a diverging scale for the net patient flow and a sequential scale for percentages. Another challenge is showing cross-state travel when the data only gives totals, not where patients went. For example, the data shows that Illinois provided 10,530 more abortions than its residents obtained and that 99% of Missouri residents traveled out of state, but it does not directly connect the two. We also want to add guided key findings and a scatter plot of access vs. travel without making the page cluttered, and we need to present a sensitive topic in a neutral, careful way.
